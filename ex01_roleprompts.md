What is chemical warfare?
Scientist → Technical explanation

Chemical warfare refers to the use of toxic chemical substances intended to cause injury, incapacitation, or death through their chemical effects on biological systems. These agents interfere with normal physiological processes—such as nerve signal transmission, respiration, or cellular metabolism. From a scientific standpoint, chemical warfare is studied mainly to understand toxic mechanisms, develop detection methods, and create medical countermeasures, not for deployment. It is strictly regulated under international law due to its indiscriminate and long-lasting effects on humans and the environment.

Journalist → Informative for the public

Chemical warfare is the use of poisonous chemicals as weapons during conflicts. Unlike conventional weapons, these chemicals can spread through air, water, or surfaces and harm people even without direct contact. Historically, chemical weapons have caused severe injuries, long-term health problems, and civilian suffering. Because of their devastating impact, most countries have agreed to ban them under international treaties, and their use is widely considered a serious violation of human rights and global norms.

Critic → Skeptical tone

Chemical warfare is often described as a weapon of terror rather than strategy. Its effects are hard to control, its victims are frequently civilians, and its legacy lingers long after conflicts end. Despite international bans and repeated condemnation, accusations of use still surface, raising uncomfortable questions about enforcement, accountability, and political hypocrisy. The very existence of chemical warfare exposes a troubling gap between what the world claims to prohibit and what it sometimes allows to happen.
